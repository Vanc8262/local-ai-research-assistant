## research / sodium-cells

**Question:** How do the Tessaly sodium-ion cells compare with the lithium iron phosphate (LFP) reference on durability, cost and performance?

**Answer:**

In the Tessaly Labs cycling study, sodium‑ion cells retained 91 % of their capacity after 2,000 cycles at 25 °C, while LFP reference cells retained 93 % under the same protocol [1]. The independent cost analysis projects a sodium‑ion pack price of $62 per kWh versus $89 per kWh for the LFP reference at projected volume [2]. In the Northern fleet field trial, the sodium‑ion cells achieved an energy density of 148 Wh/kg, which is roughly 20 % lower than the LFP reference [3]. At –20 °C, the sodium‑ion cells delivered 84 % of their rated capacity; no comparable cold‑temperature performance figure is given for the LFP reference in the sources [3].

## research / lake-orrin

**Question:** Why does Lake Orrin have an algae problem, and what is being done about it?

**Answer:**

Lake Orrin had a mean phosphorus concentration of 48 µg/L in 2023, exceeding the 30 µg/L target and experiencing algal blooms on 37 days [1]. Roughly 60% of the lake’s phosphorus load originates from agricultural runoff in the Pell Creek sub-basin [2]. The county council approved $2.3 million for buffer strips along 14 km of Pell Creek, aiming to reduce the phosphorus load by 25% within five years [3].

## research / lattice-pruning

**Question:** How well does lattice pruning work on the Brel-7 model, and what caveats have been reported?

**Answer:**

Lattice pruning removes 40% of the Brel-7 model's weights, causing a 0.8‑point accuracy loss on the Mirel benchmark [1]. An independent replication reported a 1.9‑point accuracy loss when the calibration set was reduced from 5,000 to 500 examples [2]. After pruning, inference latency on a mid‑range GPU dropped from 38 ms to 27 ms per request, and memory use fell 35% [3].
